ARXO-2026-0974 · Reported

Justice Laws: language marks in the wrong form in 11 historical notes

English historical notes mark language-specific amendments (E) and (F); French notes use (A) and (F). In 11 places of the consolidation the mark has the form of the other language: English note of the Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act, s. 49: “223(A)”; French notes with “(E)” in the Criminal Code, s. 183; Olympic and Paralympic Marks Act, ss. 3, 4 and 8; Canada Labour Code, s. 47.1; Family Orders and Agreements Enforcement Assistance Act, s. 49; Royal Canadian Mounted Police Superannuation Regulations, ss. 57 and 59; Natural Health Products Regulations, ss. 28 and 32.

Consequence

The marks remain readable; they are harder to read by machine and to search. No effect on the law.

Proposed correction

The Department of Justice decides how to correct each place in the consolidation or the XML; the evidence gives the wording of each language version.

Lois du Canada : marques de langue sous une forme erronée dans 11 notes historiques

Les notes anglaises emploient (E) et (F); les notes françaises, (A) et (F). À 11 endroits de la codification, la marque a la forme de l’autre langue, par exemple Code criminel, art. 183, note française : « ch. 20, art. 366(E) »; Loi sur le recyclage des produits de la criminalité et le financement des activités terroristes, art. 49, note anglaise : « 223(A) ».
